# Board memo — what the flags mean, and what the pack misses

**To:** CFO, Paju Consumer Products Oy **Re:** FY2025 board pack, six recurring flag clusters
**From:** Senior analyst (run 03) **Figures:** EUR, ex-VAT, from the public data tables

## The one line

The business grew revenue 8.6% (EUR 154.7M to 168.0M) and lost profit doing it. Underlying EBITDA — stripping a EUR 2.2M one-off in April — fell 13.7%, from EUR 17.96M to EUR 15.50M. Reported EBITDA looks flat (–1.4%) only because that one-off is in it. The cause is not cost; it is discounting.

## What deserves the board's attention

**1. Margin erosion is a discounting decision, not a supplier problem (Nordics, Baltics & Poland).** Company gross-to-net promotional discount stepped from ~5.8% of gross (all of 2024 and January 2025) to 8.4% in February and 10.4% by December. Unit material cost is flat and within ~1% of budget in every business unit — the recurring "material cost" flags are purely volume growth and point the board the wrong way. The real damage: Nordics Home Care discount depth tripled (6% to 19% of gross), taking that line's margin from 27.6% to 16.5% and Nordics' margin from ~43% to ~39%; Baltics & Poland discount rose 8% to 17% across every line. List prices were held throughout, so this is promotions, not price cuts. *Action: add a net-price / discount measure to the pack; review the Nordics Home Care and B&P promotional calendars.*

**2. The EBITDA-vs-budget miss is two unrelated problems.** Nordics missed budget EBITDA by EUR 2.18M — a EUR 4.23M gross-profit shortfall (the discounting above), half-hidden by the EUR 2.2M one-off. Baltics & Poland missed by EUR 0.82M for the opposite reason: gross profit actually beat budget by EUR 1.44M, but S&M ran 23% over and admin 22% over. B&P is buying volume (+30% to +46% YoY, spread evenly across all 11 customers) with discount and spend, and its EBITDA is down ~40% YoY. Central Europe is broadly on plan. *Action: manage Nordics pricing and B&P cost-to-grow separately.*

**3. Central Europe's DSO problem is one customer.** CE DSO reached 76.7 days (+23 YoY), and 87% of the receivables build is Rheinkauf Gruppe. Its balance grew from EUR 2.06M to EUR 4.49M — from 1.5 to ~3.4 months of its own sales, roughly 95–110 days against 45-day terms — while its revenue was flat (+4.4%). This is slower payment, not more sales. It also explains why operating cash flow (EUR 10.3M) trailed underlying EBITDA (EUR 15.5M): receivables absorbed EUR 3.9M, and EUR 5.5M of the EUR 15.8M cash increase was the April one-off, not operations. *Action: treat Rheinkauf's ~EUR 4.5M balance (about EUR 2.4M beyond normal) as a collection and credit-limit matter.*

## What the board can stand down on

**January's –28.7% revenue drop is the calendar.** December peaks and January troughs every year; the budget assumed the same –27% step, and January 2025 was in fact up 2.6% on January 2024 and only 1.6% below budget. Nothing changed in customer behaviour. No action.

**April's +196% EBITDA spike is a one-off, not a turnaround.** It is a single EUR 2.2M other-income entry booked to Nordics, alongside a EUR 5.5M investing inflow the same month (the only non-zero investing line in two years, consistent with an asset disposal). Underlying EBITDA that month was EUR 1.19M — the ordinary run-rate. Exclude it from trends; but note it is also what makes the full-year reported EBITDA look flat (see the one line above).

**The "material cost" flags are not a cost event.** Covered in point 1: per-unit cost is flat and on budget; the euro increase is units sold. Stand down on procurement; do not stand down on the discounting behind it.

## Net read

Three things belong on the agenda — promotional discounting (the profit story), Baltics & Poland's cost-to-grow, and the Rheinkauf receivable. Two of the six flag clusters (January, the April spike) are noise, and a third (material cost) is a real problem wearing the wrong label. The pack's gap is that it has no measure for discount or net-price realisation, which is exactly where the year was lost.
