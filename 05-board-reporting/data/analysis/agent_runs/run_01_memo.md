# Board memo — Paju Consumer Products Oy, FY2025

**To:** CFO **From:** Senior analyst **Re:** What sits behind the board-pack flags, and what the pack misses

## The one thing to take away

Revenue grew 8.6% in 2025, but reported EBITDA fell 1.4% (EUR 17.96m to EUR 17.70m) and, stripping out a EUR 2.2m one-off, underlying EBITDA fell 13.7% to EUR 15.50m. The company is buying volume with discounts. Gross margin dropped 2.31 points (42.5% to 40.2%), around EUR 3.9m of gross profit given away. Almost every flag in the pack is a symptom of this one mechanism, and the pack never names it: the promotional discount rate.

## What deserves the board's attention

**Nordics and Baltics margin, driven by discounting (clusters 2, 3, 4).** The recurring "material cost" flags are a false trail: material cost per unit is flat everywhere and, in Nordics, below budget. Total material cost rises only because volume rises. The real leak is on price. The Nordics discount rate went from 6.0% to 9.6%, concentrated entirely in Home Care (6.0% to 18.8%, on 42% more volume). Baltics went from 8.0% to 12.5% and is still climbing month by month — 8.2% in January to 17.1% in December. Central Europe held discipline at ~4% and grew EBITDA 12.4%, so this is a two-BU problem, not a company-wide one. Consequence: the EUR 3.4m reported EBITDA shortfall to budget (EUR 5.6m ex the one-off) is a Nordics gross-margin miss of EUR 4.2m plus a Baltics miss where above-budget gross profit is swallowed by S&M and admin overspend. Nordics underlying EBITDA actually fell 28.5% year on year, masked to 6.1% by the one-off. Recommendation: a gross-to-net discount bridge in the pack, and a discount ceiling per BU and line; the Baltics trajectory is the urgent part.

**Central Europe receivables — one customer (cluster 5).** The DSO worsening to ~77 days is not a CE-wide trend; it is Rheinkauf Gruppe. Its receivable tripled (EUR 2.06m to EUR 4.49m), 87% of the entire CE receivables build, and it now pays at 116 days against 45-day terms. Two Benelux distributors have stretched mildly; every other CE account is near terms. This is a credit and collections matter for one name, and it is tying up EUR 2.4m of cash. Recommendation: dunning, a credit hold, and a provision review on Rheinkauf specifically.

**Cash quality (miss).** Closing cash rose EUR 15.8m, which looks reassuring, but EUR 5.5m of that is a one-off asset disposal and operating cash conversion fell from 76% to 66% of underlying EBITDA. The growth is being financed into receivables (+EUR 3.9m, of which CE +EUR 2.8m and Baltics +EUR 1.2m). Recommendation: report operating cash conversion and keep the disposal out of the operating read.

## What to stand down on

**January's 28.7% revenue drop (cluster 1).** Seasonality, nothing more. December is the annual peak and January the trough in both years; January 2025 was up 2.6% year on year, positive at every BU. The month-on-month flag is measuring the calendar. No action.

**April's 196% EBITDA spike (cluster 6).** A single EUR 2.2m other-income entry booked to Nordics, alongside a EUR 5.5m asset-disposal inflow — the only non-zero entries of their kind all year. Underlying April margin was the usual 8.8%. Exclude it from run-rate; its only lasting effect is to flatter the reported full-year figure and hide the Nordics decline.

## Also worth a line

Growth quality: the full-year bridge shows all EUR 13.3m of revenue growth is volume (+EUR 22.2m), with price (-EUR 3.0m) and mix (-EUR 5.9m) pulling the other way. Within Nordics the mix shift is a trade-down — the high-value Skin & Body line is shrinking 13% while discounted Home Care grows 42%. Worth asking why Skin & Body is losing volume before deciding whether the Home Care discount is defending share or destroying value.
