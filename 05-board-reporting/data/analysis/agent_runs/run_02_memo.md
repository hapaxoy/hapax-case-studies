# Board memo — FY2025 variance review

**To:** CFO, Paju Consumer Products Oy · **From:** Analysis · **Re:** what sits behind the flags

The pack's flags are accurate but point at the wrong cause. The story of 2025 is a single decision: from February, promotional discounting was stepped up. Revenue beat budget by EUR6.0m; underlying EBITDA came in EUR5.6m below it. The gap is margin given away at the till, not cost, not volume.

## What deserves the board's attention

**Discounting has cut EUR5.9m of margin (F3, F8).** The company discount rate roughly doubled through 2025 — from about 5.9% of gross (steady all through 2024) to 10.4% by December — and gross margin broke in the same month, February. Held at 2024 rates, the company would have kept EUR5.9m: EUR3.6m of it in Nordics, EUR2.3m in Baltics & Poland, none in Central Europe. That EUR5.9m more than covers the entire EBITDA shortfall to budget. Material cost per unit is flat to slightly down and on budget in every unit and every line — the "material cost" flags are simply higher volume, not inflation. The pack reports by business unit, which hides where this lands: Home Care, the lowest-margin line at ~21%, took 71% of the extra discount and gained share of volume at the same time. Nordics Home Care went from 6.0% to 18.8% of gross discounted in one month. Recommend: put net-price policy on the agenda, quantify the return on the extra EUR5.9m of promotion, and set a price floor by line.

**Baltics & Poland is growing at a loss to itself (F5).** Revenue rose 27% but underlying EBITDA fell 8%, from EUR3.21m to EUR2.95m. The volume was bought twice over — with a discount climbing to 17% of gross by December, and with sales & marketing EUR1.3m (23%) and admin EUR0.9m (22%) over budget. The top-line flags read as good news; the economics do not. Recommend: hold B&P opex to budget and test whether the volume survives a lower discount.

**One customer is behind the Central Europe DSO (F6).** Central Europe DSO worsening to ~77 days is not a regional trend — it is Rheinkauf Gruppe, the unit's largest account on 45-day terms. Its overdue balance more than doubled, from EUR2.06m to EUR4.49m, about four months of its own sales outstanding, while its collections fell EUR1.7m year on year. Every other CE account sits near two months and on terms. This is concentration and credit risk. Recommend: escalate to collection and review the credit limit.

## What does not — stand down

- **January's 28.7% drop (F1)** is the December-to-January seasonal reset. December is the annual peak in both years; January the trough. Against the prior January, revenue grew 2.6%. Report it year on year.
- **Rising material cost (F2)** is volume, not price. Unit cost is on or below budget everywhere.
- **The Baltics & Poland top line (F4)** is genuine unit-volume growth, not a distortion. The concern is its margin (F5), not its size.
- **April's EBITDA spike of 196% (F7)** is a EUR2.2m one-off other-income item in Nordics, matched by a EUR5.5m investing inflow the same month — consistent with an asset disposal. Strip it out and April was 28% under budget, like every other month. It should not be read as operating improvement.

## Two things the pack does not surface

- **The budget runs hot (F9).** EBITDA missed budget by 8.2% in 2024 as well, before any discounting. About a third of the 2025 gap is a standing planning bias, not a new event; separate the two when judging the year and recalibrate 2026.
- **Cash conversion is weakening (F10).** Operating cash flow fell EUR3.4m year on year as receivables built EUR3.9m. Closing cash of EUR37.5m looks strong, but EUR5.5m of the year's rise is the April one-off. Track operating cash excluding one-offs alongside EBITDA.

**Bottom line:** revenue is not the problem and cost is not the problem. Price discipline is. The Nordics and Home Care discount and the Rheinkauf receivable are the two items worth board time; the rest is seasonality, volume, and a one-off.
