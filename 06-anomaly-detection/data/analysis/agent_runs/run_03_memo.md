# Purchase ledger exception review — FY2025

Two automated layers raised 64 rule flags and 200 detector flags, grouped into 39 units. I have verdicted every unit and run the standard analyst sweeps behind them. Twelve units are worth your attention; the rest stand down on the numbers. Two things want action this week.

The first is a vendor-master defect the rules could not see. One German seal supplier — VAT DE199283746 — is set up three times, as V-0003, V-0004 and V-0005, under three spellings ('Kaerrenbach', 'Karrenbach', 'KARRENBACH'), and each record carries a different IBAN. The three together took EUR 48,001 across 15 invoices, all seals (account 4110). One legal entity paid into three bank accounts is both a spend-visibility problem and a payment-diversion vector. The name_hygiene test missed it because it keys on byte-identical names, which three different spellings defeat; keying on VAT exposes it at once. Please merge the three records and confirm each IBAN belongs to the real supplier before the next payment run.

The second is freight vendor V-0012 (Ratashuolto Oy). Its four invoices for the year are EUR 9,580.10, 9,650.75, 9,712.45 and 9,899.99 — every one inside EUR 420 of the EUR 10,000 department-head approval limit, on a tidy quarterly cadence, never crossing it. It is the only vendor in the whole ledger sitting in the 9,500–9,999.99 band, while 51 other invoices cross EUR 10,000 openly. That consistency just under the line is the signature of pricing kept below approval. I would put these freight charges through department-head review regardless and ask the buyer why they cluster where they do.

The remaining worries are watch-level. Advertising agency V-0006 has all eight of its campaign invoices (memo 'Mainoskampanja', EUR 36,934) booked by U-204 to IT services (7200) rather than its only allowed account, Marketing (7100) — a clean misclassification to reclassify. Three tiny vendors (V-0008, V-0009, V-0010) each bill the same purchase order twice at an identical amount days apart under plain 'Ostolasku' memos; if these are re-bills rather than genuine repeat deliveries, EUR 10,664 is recoverable, and V-0018's twin EUR 7,420.00 invoices deserve the same check. Three more small vendors (V-0013, V-0014, V-0015) show the split pattern — two sub-EUR-10,000 invoices one to three days apart summing above the limit; they are the only vendors in the ledger that do this, the memos say 'osatoimitus' (partial delivery), so confirm whether each pair is really one procurement that should have carried approval. V-0011's steel invoices are exact round thousands every two months (EUR 41,000 total), atypical for weight-priced steel and worth a contract spot-check, though nothing is split or near a threshold. Finally, user U-117 is the only AP clerk who posts on weekends without a stated reason — eight small, ordinary invoices, against zero for every peer; most likely someone working Saturdays, but worth one confirmation that the access is authorised.

What I stood down. The monthly EUR 15,000 rent (V-0016) and U-031's Saturday postings are plainly benign — the rent is rent, and U-031's memos state an agreed stocktake on 15 November. All 24 detector-vendor clusters, the ten SINGLES and the 30-line RESIDUAL tail are the Isolation Forest doing its job on the upper and rarer end of normal distributions: every flagged amount sits inside its own vendor's history, every account is allowed, and none tripped a rule test. The top-ranked anomaly in the ledger (EUR 3,721.96, V-0135) is large only against that vendor's EUR 775 median. The Nov/Dec clustering of detector flags simply follows the ledger's real seasonality — a year-end stocking peak and a July trough visible across every account. Two discretionary one-offs in SINGLES — EUR 7,576 representation and EUR 8,465 consulting — are worth an eyeball for paperwork, nothing more.

What I did not look at. Only the seven supplied files; I did not open the answer key, design notes or other runs. I could not verify that the 51 over-EUR-10,000 invoices actually received sign-off (approvals are not in the data), nor confirm VAT numbers against an external registry — both are checks for your team rather than the ledger.
